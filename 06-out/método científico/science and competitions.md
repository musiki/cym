## historical

Modern scientific competitions descend from early prize-based epistemology and were institutionalized after WWII to objectively measure progress in complex, theory-poor domains, making them especially suited to AI, biology, and systems where understanding must be demonstrated through performance rather than explanation.



- Longitude Prize (UK, 1714): navigation accuracy problem
- Académie des Sciences prize problems (France, 18th c.)
- Mathematical challenges (Bernoulli family, Euler, Fermat)

### **AI and computation**
- ImageNet Challenge (vision)
- GLUE / SuperGLUE (language)
- DARPA Grand Challenges (autonomous vehicles)
- Kaggle competitions (applied ML)
### **Biology and chemistry**
- CASP (protein structure)
- CAPRI (protein–protein docking)
- DREAM Challenges (systems biology)
- CAGI (genome interpretation)
### **Robotics and embodied systems**
- RoboCup
- DARPA Robotics Challenge
### **Astronomy and physics**
- Kaggle cosmology challenges
- LIGO mock data challenges
- Solar flare prediction competitions
